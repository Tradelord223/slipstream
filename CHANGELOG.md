# Changelog

## 0.5.0 (2026-10-21)

- **Mail sorts itself, and says why.** Every conversation is now sorted on your computer, with no AI, into Important, Updates, Promotions, Social or Forums, and keeps the reason ("You've written to them", "Gmail filed it under Promotions", "It has an unsubscribe link"). The first rule that applies wins: you wrote in the conversation or the sender is a VIP; your own correction for the sender, company or mailing list; someone you've written to; your provider's own sorting (Gmail's Promotions, Social, Updates, Forums and Personal tabs, and Outlook's Focused); headers and wording (social networks, sign-in codes, deliveries, receipts, discussion lists, account and service addresses such as `notifications@`, `billing@` and `orders@`, unsubscribe links); and otherwise, a person. A receipt or an account notice that carries an unsubscribe link stays in Updates, and a store that writes under a person's name still lands in Promotions. This fixes stores filling Important: mail stored before 0.5.0 has no List-Id or provider category, so the old rule called it a person's; a one-time re-sort (below) fetches those headers.
- **Your tabs change when you upgrade.** The built-in Important and Other tabs are replaced by Important, Updates, Promotions and Social, plus Forums, which shows only while it holds mail (it is set to **hide when empty**, so you won't see it until a mailing-list discussion arrives). Tabs you made yourself stay, after them. Every built-in tab can be renamed, hidden, set to hide when empty (every tab has that option now), and moved, but not deleted. To hide one, open **Settings → Splits** and turn off **Show as a tab**, or use `splits.update` with `hidden`. A tab you hide no longer pulls mail out of the built-in tabs. Removing a tab you made now has **Undo**, which puts it back in the same place.
- **Older mail is sorted again, once.** The first time 0.5.0 runs, it goes back over mail it stored before, in the background, a little at a time: it asks your mail server again for the four header lines that say a message is from a list or a machine (about 1 KB a message, by UID on IMAP; message metadata on Gmail and Outlook) and for Gmail's or Outlook's own category, then sorts every conversation. It never downloads a message body, never changes what is done, trashed or in your inbox, and writes nothing to your mail account. If it is interrupted it carries on where it stopped. When it finishes, Activity has one line, **Re-sorted 8,600 conversations**, which can't be undone because it only changes which tab mail shows in. `slipstream doctor` shows **Sorting: re-sorting 42%** until it is done.
- **Gmail's and Outlook's own sorting is used.** Gmail's Promotions, Social, Updates and Forums tabs (over the API, and over IMAP with one search per tab for each batch of new mail) and Outlook's Focused now count when Slipstream sorts. Outlook's Other says nothing on its own: a person's mail in it stays Important. Gmail's Personal counts as Important only when no machine sent the mail, so receipts in Gmail's Primary tab don't stay there. Other IMAP servers (Fastmail, iCloud) have no categories, so they are sorted by headers and wording alone.
- **Move a sender's mail for good.** Agents (and the dashboard) can send a sender, a company or a mailing list to another tab with `mail.recategorize`. What is already in your inbox moves at once, the correction shows in Activity and can be undone, and `categoryRules.list` and `categoryRules.delete` show and remove them. A correction beats "someone you've written to", so a store you once emailed can still go to Promotions; a conversation you wrote in stays in Important.
- **Tabs worth making are suggested.** `splits.suggest` counts the last 30 days of your inbox and offers mailing lists, companies, calendar invites and receipts that have at least 8 conversations and that none of your tabs already hold. It is plain counting, with no model; a dismissed suggestion doesn't come back.
- **Describe a tab in words.** With ChatGPT or an API key connected, a description such as "mail about my kids' school" becomes a search you can read and edit, and Slipstream shows how many conversations it finds and a few of their subjects before anything is saved. Only your description goes to the model, never your mail. It counts toward your monthly agent budget and stops when you cancel it. With no model connected there is nothing to turn on.
- **New search words.** `category:people|updates|promotions|social|forums`, `domain:shop.com`, `list:news.shop.com` and `is:bulk`. `category:important`, `news`, `notification` and `other` still work. **`is:noreply` changed meaning:** it used to mean "nobody has replied" and now means mail from a no-reply address; `is:unanswered` has the old meaning, and your saved tabs and auto-label rules that used `is:noreply` were rewritten to it. See [docs/agent-api.md](docs/agent-api.md#search-words-and-how-mail-is-sorted).
- **Why it's there, and a key to fix it.** Open a conversation and a small chip under the subject says where it was sorted and why ("Promotions · It has an unsubscribe link", "Gmail filed it under Promotions"). Click it, or press **`Shift+I`** on any conversation in the list or open, to move that sender's mail to another tab: choose the sender, everyone at the company or the mailing list, then Important, Updates, Promotions, Social or Forums (keys 1 to 5). What is already in your inbox moves at once and a toast offers **Undo**. **Settings → Splits** lists these corrections so you can remove them.
- **New split from a conversation.** Press **`Shift+N`** (or **Cmd+K → New split from this conversation**) to choose what to split out: the sender, the company, the mailing list, one of its labels or someone else on it. Each choice shows how many conversations it would hold, **Enter** makes the split named for your choice, and the name can be changed first.
- **A quiet suggestion at the end of the tabs.** "Split out GitHub? 23 conversations · Add · ✕" offers one worth making at a time and never more than one new suggestion a day. Dismissing one is remembered, and the **+** dialog lists all of them above the ready-made splits. With a model connected, the dialog also has a box to describe a split in words.
- **Tabs you can tidy.** Tab and Shift+Tab skip hidden tabs and tabs that hide when empty. In **Settings → Splits** every tab, built-in or not, has **Show as a tab** and **Hide when empty** switches, built-in tabs can be renamed and moved, and removing a tab you made offers **Undo**.
- **`slipstream connect` uses the copy that starts at login.** When `slipstream service install` has set Slipstream up, `connect claude` and `connect codex` (and Settings → Connect) point at the service's own copy under your data folder instead of npx's cache, so Claude Code and Codex keep working offline and with npm's minimum-release-age setting. `service uninstall` removes that copy, so it now tells you to run `connect` again.
- **The MCP server reports its real version** instead of `0.1.0`.
- **Fixed:** sale emails that talk about orders "arriving by Friday" showed an in-transit delivery chip. From a mailing list, a delivery now needs the status in the subject, a tracking number or a carrier. Chips on mail you already have are read again when 0.5.0 starts.

## 0.4.0 (2026-10-20)

- **Keep Slipstream running.** `slipstream service install` makes Slipstream start when you log in and start again if it stops, so mail keeps syncing and Claude Code or Codex can always reach it. `service status` shows whether it is running and where its log is, and `service uninstall` removes it. On macOS it is a LaunchAgent; on Linux it is a systemd user service (tested with stand-ins, not yet on a Linux machine). Run through `npx`, it first copies Slipstream out of npx's cache, which npm can clear, into `<data folder>/service/`, so the service never needs the network to start. Run `service install` again after upgrading; an upgrade that doesn't come up goes back to the version that was running. The service keeps the settings you started Slipstream with, such as your own Google or Microsoft app and API keys, in a file only you can read, and its log is private too. It refuses to run while `SLIPSTREAM_SECRET` is set, because it can't use that yet. Windows isn't supported yet.
- **Its own window and Dock icon.** Slipstream can now be installed as an app from Chrome (the **Install** button in the address bar) and has a proper icon for Safari's **File → Add to Dock**.
- **Fixed:** starting Slipstream while another copy was already running began syncing mail before failing with an unexplained error. It now checks that it has its port before it touches your mail, and says "Slipstream is already running at …" if it doesn't.
- **Docs:** the 0.3.0 note on Codex said sub-agents were off; Slipstream asks Codex to turn them off, but Codex 0.163.0 still lists their tools.

## 0.3.0 (2026-10-20)

- **Security: Codex runs no longer get your Codex plugins' tools.** When your own Codex runs Autopilot or the assistant, Slipstream now also turns off Codex plugins and apps, memories, image generation, goals, skills, browser use and computer use, and asks Codex to turn off sub-agents. (In testing with Codex 0.163.0, its sub-agent tools were still listed, though Codex runs read-only with no shell.) Before this, MCP servers that came from Codex plugins could start alongside Slipstream's tools, so a mail agent could reach, for example, a connected trading or database plugin. If Slipstream can't get the list of features from `codex features list`, it doesn't start Codex and tells you; the list is remembered until Codex is upgraded. Codex still reads your global `~/.codex/AGENTS.md`.
- **`slipstream connect claude` and `slipstream connect codex`** print the command that adds Slipstream to Claude Code or Codex over the stdio bridge, with no token to copy; `--run` runs it for you. The command uses the `node` on your PATH rather than a versioned install path, so it survives a Node upgrade, and Settings → Connect shows the same one. Run through `npx slipstream-mail`, it runs `npx -y slipstream-mail@<version> mcp`, pinned to the version you are running. Claude Code and Codex added this way share one "Local MCP" agent. The README now leads with this. See [docs/agent-api.md](docs/agent-api.md#claude-code-and-codex-in-one-command).
- **No-reply addresses are called out.** A draft addressed to `no-reply@`, `noreply@` or `do-not-reply@` (in To, Cc or Bcc) now shows a quiet line under the recipients, "no-reply@example.com doesn't accept replies.", and agents get the same line in a `warnings` field on `drafts.create` and `drafts.update`, and on a `drafts.send` that runs. A send that waits for approval shows the line in its Review preview instead. Nothing is blocked. Autopilot no longer drafts a reply to one and notes why on its decision.
- **Fixed:** when a lot of new mail arrived at once, Autopilot could stop with "Autopilot is already busy." and pause sorting for 10 minutes while it was still drafting replies to earlier mail. New mail now waits for a free slot and is sorted as soon as a drafting run finishes.
- **Search reaches all your mail.** Slipstream keeps summaries of the last 30 days, so searches now also ask each account's provider (Gmail, Outlook or your IMAP server) for older mail and for words deep in a message. Your local results appear at once; a quiet line at the end says "Searching all your mail at Gmail…", the finds merge in newest first, and **Show more** pages on. An account that can't be searched says so with **Try again**, and the others still answer. Finds are stored as ordinary conversations, summary only, and open like any other. Agents get `mail.searchAll`, which never returns a conversation with a one-time code when the search has words. See [docs/agent-api.md](docs/agent-api.md#searching-all-of-a-persons-mail).
- **A cap on the mail cache, and a faster recent cache.** Slipstream keeps every summary but full text only for the newest 50 inbox messages and what you opened recently, up to 100 MB (`cacheLimitMb` in settings). Past that, the least recently read mail goes back to previews and its downloaded attachment files are deleted unless a draft, a logo or an imported message still uses them. Mail that can't be fetched again (imports, mail you sent that hasn't synced back, the demo) is never removed. `slipstream doctor` shows the cache size. An install that grew under the old sync (one grew to 720 MB) is trimmed once at startup in the background and, if that frees a lot, the database file is compacted by a VACUUM in a worker thread.
- **The recent cache is one request over IMAP:** the newest 50 inbox messages come in one `FETCH` instead of 50, and only the newest 300 messages of history get previews (in one pass); older history keeps an empty snippet until opened.
- **Faster first sync over IMAP.** The inbox opens on headers alone, then the newest 50 messages arrive in full in one request, and only the messages that still need it get their first lines (Gmail refuses one request that asks for parts some messages lack, so that is one request per kind of message). Sync progress now includes a `previews` stage and the rate so far, for a time-remaining estimate.
- **A real first run.** A new install opens a short setup instead of an invented mailbox. You enter your email, and Slipstream works out where it lives from the domain's own DNS, so Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 addresses on custom domains are recognised. It then walks you through the right sign-in: guided app-password steps for Gmail, iCloud, Yahoo and Fastmail, Microsoft sign-in for Outlook, or server settings only when you need them. A live count shows mail arriving, and your inbox opens as soon as the first messages land. An optional last step adds another account or chooses AI: none, your own agent, or the built-in assistant.
- **Settings → Accounts** is simpler: your accounts and one Add account button that opens the same steps.
- **The invented demo mailbox is for developers only:** `npm run dev`, `SLIPSTREAM_DEMO=1` or `slipstream --demo`. Outside developer demo mode a new install doesn't create one. If you upgrade from a version that gave you one, it is removed when you connect a real account; in developer demo mode it stays beside your real accounts.
- **"Continue with Microsoft" needs your install's Microsoft app** for now (see [docs/connect-accounts.md](docs/connect-accounts.md#register-slipstreams-microsoft-app)) until Slipstream ships its own. Google works the same way, with your install's Google app.
- **Fixed:** removing an account that had synced mail failed with an error.
- **Fixed:** removing an account while its mail file was still being imported stopped the whole server. The import now ends with a plain note, and the server keeps running. The first sync after connecting an account and each import log their own failures instead of leaving them to close Slipstream.
- **Reconnect:** an account that needs to sign in again, or couldn't sync, has a Reconnect button in Settings → Accounts, and the rail's "Reconnect" link goes to it. Gmail and Outlook start their own sign-in; a password account opens its sign-in with the address filled in, so the new password updates the same account.
- **Mail servers found in a domain's own settings** are always shown, with a short note, before you enter a password.
- **IMAP accounts remember their provider**, so iCloud, Fastmail, Yahoo, Zoho and Proton accounts are labelled as such instead of "Email account" (accounts connected before this show it after they're reconnected).
- **Two imports into one account** no longer clash: the account keeps filling until the last one ends.
- **Doctor changes nothing:** it no longer creates the data folder or leaves `-wal` and `-shm` files next to the database.
- **Fixed:** a demo mailbox left from an older version is removed at startup once you have a real account.
- **Imports finish properly:** an imported file's account is no longer marked as "first synced" before its messages have arrived, and the app reports how far an import has got after a reload.
- **Sign-in errors say which provider** (Google or Microsoft) they came from.
- **Address lookup is more careful:** only domains that Hotmail, Outlook, Live and Yahoo really run are assumed, anything else is looked up; Zoho addresses in the EU, India and Australia get their own region's servers; the limit on simultaneous lookups is per person as well as overall; and a lookup that times out is cancelled.
- **Docs:** the account steps now use the current names (**Add account**, **Continue with Google**, **Continue with Microsoft**, **Import a mail file**), and the Google Workspace app password note follows Google's own help.
- `slipstream doctor` checks an install without changing anything and explains each problem in plain words: Node.js, data folder permissions, disk space, database health and version, whether the encryption key still opens saved sign-ins, whether Slipstream or another program holds its port, each mail account's sync, and changes or sends that didn't go through. On team servers it also checks the https public address, backups and `SLIPSTREAM_SECRET`. `--json` for scripts and agents; exit code 1 when something needs fixing.

## 0.2.0 (2026-10-16)

**Everyday helpers, with no AI needed**
- One-time codes get a Copy chip in the list and a card in the conversation; `Shift+C` copies without opening the email. Codes are masked in everything agents and Autopilot read.
- Package updates show their status (shipped, out for delivery, delivered and more). When a newer update for the same package arrives, the older ones move to Done, and you can undo it.
- Sign-in alerts and urgent mail from people (legal notices, overdue bills, deadlines) are flagged and never moved automatically.
- New search operators: `is:code`, `is:delivery`, `is:security`, `is:urgent`. Settings → Helpers turns each helper on or off and adds matching splits in one click.

**Send from any of your addresses**
- Gmail send-as aliases are read automatically. Add custom-domain, Outlook and IMAP addresses by hand, each with its own name, reply-to and signature.
- The composer's From picker lists every address, and replies default to the address the email was sent to.
- The server checks that every From belongs to the account, for people and agents alike. Mail with your alias in its From header counts as yours only when your provider files it as sent.

**AI off by default, and a Privacy & safety page**
- The built-in assistant and Autopilot stay hidden until you turn them on in Settings → AI & agents. Off is enforced by the server: Autopilot and routines pause too, and switching back on starts from that moment rather than catching up. Your own agents can connect over MCP and the CLI either way.
- Settings → Privacy & safety says in plain words how email is shown, what is stored and encrypted, and what any model sees.
- `npm run test:render` renders 19 hostile emails in headless Chrome and fails if any run script or reach the network. It found and fixed one leak: quoted background images in replies and forwards.

**Company logos (off by default)**
- Logos for company senders, fetched by the computer running Slipstream at a random time about once a month, never by your browser. Requests are restricted to public https addresses, logos are checked by their contents and SVGs are reduced to plain shapes.

**Project**
- New README, logo, contributing guide, security policy, code of conduct and issue templates.

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-15)

First public release: a keyboard-first, local-first email client for Gmail, Outlook and any IMAP server, with split inbox, reminders, Send Later and Undo Send, snippets, labels and teams, plus agents over MCP, the HTTP API, the CLI and webhooks, a managed cloud agent, Autopilot, and agents on any model (ChatGPT sign-in, Claude Code, Codex, or Claude, OpenAI, Gemini and OpenRouter keys).
